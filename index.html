One-Syllable Word Search

Find one-syllable English words by their sounds. Fill in any part of the syllable in IPA and leave the rest blank to match anything.

    How to search
    • Type sounds, not spelling. The onset of phone is [f], and the onset of knee is [n].
    • Square brackets [ ] and slashes / / are ignored, so you can type [pl], /pl/ or just pl.
    • A filled-in box must match that whole part of the syllable. Onset [pl] finds play but not split, whose onset is [spl].
    • Use * for “any number of sounds, including none.” Onset [*l] finds every onset that ends in [l].
    • Use ∅ (or 0) for “nothing here.” Coda [∅] finds words that end in a vowel.
    • The vowel in words like fur, bird and first is the r-colored vowel [ɝ]. Search for it in the nucleus box, not as a vowel plus [ɹ]. These words have no [ɹ] in the coda: fur is [fɝ] with no coda, and bird is [bɝd] with coda [d]. [ɚ] is searched the same way as [ɝ].
    • You may type [r] or [ɹ], and [a] or [ɑ]; each pair is searched the same way. [e] is read as [eɪ], and [o] finds both [o] (as in or) and [oʊ] (as in go).
    About the word list

    Pronunciations come from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, which describes American English. Only monosyllabic words (words with one syllable) are included.

    Some things to know:

    • Words like bird and nerve are listed with the r-colored vowel [ɝ] as their nucleus, so their codas are [d] and [v]. Words like car and fire are listed with [ɹ] in the coda. The vowel before [ɹ] in words like or, more and four is written [o].
    • Some words have more than one pronunciation, such as route as [ɹut] or [ɹaʊt]. Each one is searched, and a word appears if any of its pronunciations matches.
    • By default, names, abbreviations and very rare words are hidden. Turning them on adds words like vroom, Schwartz and Gwen, which can show onsets and codas that ordinary English words don’t use.
    • Results are listed from most to least common.
